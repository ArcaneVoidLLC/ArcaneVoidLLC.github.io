The short version. Backgammon is a game played against a program on your device. There is no wagering, no currency to buy, and no way to spend money on a better position. The dice are committed before each game starts and revealed after it ends, so either side can check them.

4. The dice

Before each game the Software draws a 32-byte seed from the device's cryptographic random source and seals it, showing you the seal before you spin. Your spin of the dial becomes a salt. The commitment for the game is SHA-256 of the seed and the salt together, and roll t is derived from HMAC-SHA-256 of the seed over the salt and t, read as two dice by rejection sampling so that every face is equally likely.

We warrant that within a game:

  • Every roll is fixed before the first checker moves, and the commitment shown to you binds it.
  • The opponent program receives a roll only when you do. It is never given the seed, the salt, or a future roll, and no roll is chosen, redrawn or weighted by the level you selected or by who is ahead.
  • The seed is revealed when the game ends, so every roll and both hashes can be recomputed and checked independently.

Lessons are the exception, and the Software says so where they are played: a lesson's dice are scripted to demonstrate a rule. Every ordinary game uses the committed dice described above.

5. No gambling

The Software offers no wagering, no stake, no prize, and no currency of any kind. The doubling cube multiplies the points of the game being played and nothing else. Nothing you buy affects a position, a roll, or a result.

6. Opponent levels

The opponent levels describe measured playing strength — the average error the program makes per decision against a reference — and not a human rating from any federation or club. A level is a description of the program's play, not a promise about yours.
